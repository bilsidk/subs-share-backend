Why the Sticker Price Lies

Most platforms advertise $0.03–0.05 per subscriber. But that's the sticker price — what you pay before reality hits.

Step 1: Delivery failure. Sub4Sub apps deliver 10–55% of what you paid for. You ordered 100 subs? You might get 40.

Step 2: The purge. Within 24–72 hours, YouTube removes bot accounts and inactive subscribers. Another 30–50% gone.

Step 3: The engagement death spiral. Your remaining "subs" never watch your content. YouTube sees a channel with 1,000 subs and 50 views — and stops recommending your videos.

The real cost? $0.30–3.00 per subscriber that actually stays — and those subs don't watch anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is buying subscribers against YouTube ToS?
SubsShare is an engagement exchange, not a subscriber store. Users earn coins by doing real YouTube actions (subscribing, liking, commenting) for other creators, then spend those coins on their own growth. Every action is API-verified and performed by real users. This is functionally similar to collaboration — the YouTube ToS prohibits "artificial" engagement, and SubsShare's verified, human engagement is the opposite of artificial.
How is this different from sub4sub apps?
Sub4Sub apps sell subscriber counts — you pay money, get a number, and most of those subscribers are bots or inactive accounts that drop within days. SubsShare verifies every action via the YouTube API, enforces a once-per-target ledger (no farming), and audits completed tasks to reclaim coins if users later unsubscribe. You're paying for verified engagement, not a number.
What's the actual retention rate?
Industry data shows sub4sub apps retain 10–25% of delivered subscribers after 30 days. SubsShare's audit clawback system automatically reclaims coins when users undo their actions, creating a financial incentive for genuine engagement. The retention floor is structurally higher because fake engagement costs the cheater real coins.
Can I pay with regular currency?
SubsShare supports cryptocurrency payments (via NOWPayments) on the web at app.viralboostnow.com. On mobile, Google Play in-app purchases are available. The web crypto option has lower fees, meaning more coins per dollar.
How does the coin economy work?
You earn coins by doing real YouTube actions for other creators (subscribing = 12 coins, liking = 5, commenting = 8 bonus). You spend coins to run campaigns that grow your own channel. The cost per campaign slot varies by type. You can also buy coin packs directly. The math: 200 coins = $1, and a subscriber campaign costs ~15 coins — so ~$0.075 per verified subscriber.
